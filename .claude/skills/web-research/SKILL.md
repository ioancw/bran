# Web research playbook

When asked to research something, follow this procedure rather than answering from memory:

1. **Reformulate the question** into 2–3 focused search queries that target different angles. Avoid generic phrasing.
2. **Search** with `WebSearch` — review titles and snippets; pick the 3–5 most credible-looking results.
3. **Read** the chosen pages in full with `WebFetch`. Don't stop at the snippet.
4. **Triangulate** — note where sources agree, where they disagree, and which claims are unsupported.
5. **Write** the answer as:
   - A one-sentence TL;DR.
   - A short body with inline `(Source: <domain>)` markers for each non-trivial claim.
   - A `Sources` section listing the full URLs at the end.
6. **Flag uncertainty** explicitly. If sources contradict each other, say so. If you couldn't verify a claim, say so.

Prefer first-party / official sources (vendor docs, government data, primary news outlets) over aggregators or AI-generated rewrites.
